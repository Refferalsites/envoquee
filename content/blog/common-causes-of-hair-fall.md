**Short answer:** the most common causes are genetics (pattern hair loss), stress or illness, nutrition gaps such as low iron, hormonal changes, and harsh styling. A dermatologist can tell which one applies to you.

## 1. Genetics

Pattern hair loss runs in families. In men it usually shows as a receding hairline or thinning crown. In women it often shows as a widening parting.

## 2. Stress, illness and big life events

A high fever, dengue, surgery, a stressful exam season or a new baby can trigger noticeable shedding a few months later. This type often settles by itself.

## 3. Nutrition

Low iron, low vitamin D and not enough protein are common in Pakistan and can affect hair. Don't self-prescribe supplements; a simple blood test tells you what you actually need.

## 4. Hormones and thyroid

Thyroid problems, PCOS and hormonal shifts after pregnancy can all show up as hair changes.

## 5. Styling and pulling

Tight ponytails, buns under a hijab every day, frequent straightening and chemical treatments can damage hair and the hairline over time.

## When to see a doctor quickly

- Round, smooth bald patches
- Hair coming out in clumps
- Painful, red or scaly scalp
- Hair loss with weight change, tiredness or irregular periods

## Where a scalp routine fits

A good routine keeps the scalp clean and cared for and is designed to support healthier, fuller-looking hair. It works best alongside medical advice when there is an underlying cause.
